## The short version

If you read nothing else, read these five lines. Everything after them is
the evidence.

1. **Your link binds on first sight.** Whoever clicks a referral link first
   owns that visitor for 60 days. A later link cannot take them away.
2. **Bound is not verified.** Clicking and signing up puts a person on your
   board. Only a signup that survives the house's verification check adds
   to your score.
3. **Every signup waits a day.** Each signup is decided once its own
   24-hour review hold, counted from the moment it bound, has run out.
4. **The board you see is a draft.** The live count is provisional. The
   result comes only from the final check that runs for 30 hours after the
   deadline.
5. **Real people, on their own networks, who actually do something.**
   That is the whole test, in one sentence. Everything the check rejects is
   a variation on failing it.

## The journey of one signup

Think of a signup as travelling through four gates. It has to pass all
four before it is worth a point.

| Stage | What happens | What can stop it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First touch | A visitor's browser sees your code for the first time. The binding lasts 60 days and a later link does not replace it. | Someone else's link reached them first. |
| 2. Binding | They create a new account while your code is held. The board's Signups column goes up by one. | The account is created outside the competition window, or after the deadline. |
| 3. Review hold | A 24-hour hold runs from the binding. Nothing is decided until it closes. | Nothing — this stage only takes time. |
| 4. Verification | The house's referral check looks at the signup: is it a distinct person, on a distinct network, who took part, and not a bot? | Same person, same network, IP cluster, idle account, bot, or a rejected referral reward. |

Only a signup that clears stage 4 is a **verified signup**, and only
verified signups are the score. The board shows both numbers as the
competition runs — every signup bound to the entry, and the verified count
as the check works through them — but the bound number is never the result.
The result is the count the final check has verified by the time it ends.

## The three clocks

Three different time spans govern this bounty, and confusing them is the
most common way to lose points you thought you had.

### Clock 1 — 60 days: first touch

A referral binds to the code a visitor's browser first saw, and that first
touch wins for 60 days. If a person clicked your link on 17 November and
creates their account on 20 November, the signup is yours. If they clicked
someone else's link first, no amount of sharing your link afterwards will
move them. There is no way to overwrite a first touch — the only move is
to be first, which is why the link belongs in front of real people as
early as possible, not saved for the last day.

### Clock 2 — 24 hours: the review hold

Every signup is decided only once its own 24-hour review hold, counted
from binding, has closed. A signup still inside its hold when the final
check ends does not count — not because it failed, but because it was
never decided. Nothing is approved for lack of evidence. The hold is per
signup, so the timing of each person matters individually: someone who
signs up on 21 November at 09:00 UTC has a hold that closes on 22
November at 09:00 UTC, inside the final check. Someone who signs up one
minute before the deadline has a hold that closes one minute before the
final check ends. A worked example: a friend clicks your link on Friday
morning, signs up on Friday afternoon, and their hold closes Saturday
afternoon — decided well before the final check ends on Sunday. A friend
who clicks on Saturday, signs up Sunday morning just before the 10:30 UTC
deadline, and whose hold closes Monday morning, is decided only if the
final check is still running then. Bring people early and the hold is a
formality; bring them at the last minute and it is a race.

### Clock 3 — 30 hours: the final check

The competition opened on **18 November 2026 at 10:30 UTC** and entries
close on **21 November 2026 at 10:30 UTC (17:30 WIB)**. The final check
starts at the deadline with no pause and runs for 30 hours. Only the
signups verified by the end of those 30 hours count. The final leaderboard
is published once every entry's re-check has a verdict, and the result is
due **6 December 2026 at 16:30 UTC**.

A signup counts when the account is created inside the window — after the
opening and before the deadline — with your code bound to it. A signup
bound after the deadline does not count, and a signup created before the
opening does not count either, even if the link was clicked early. A first
touch from before the opening still counts, as long as the account itself
is created inside the window.

## What the check rejects

The verification check is the same system that pays make.fun's ordinary
referral rewards. The bounty does not run a softer version of it. These
are the signups it refuses, and why:

- **Your own account.** A signup that is the referrer's own account never
  verifies. Making a second account through your own link is not a
  signup at all — and one account per person is a disqualification rule
  in the Terms of Service as well as in the kit's rules.
- **The same person twice.** A signup that shares its person with the
  referrer — the same human behind a different account — does not count.
- **The same network.** A signup that shares the referrer's network does
  not count. Accounts created from the same household or office network
  as the referrer look like one person, not a crowd. This is the rule
  that quietly kills the most "clever" plans.
- **An IP cluster.** A burst of signups from one IP address is treated as
  a single cluster, and the cluster does not verify. Ten signups from
  one address are worth the same as zero — and they also mark the
  referrer.
- **An idle account.** An account that signs up and then does nothing
  never verifies. Verification wants a participant, not a row in a
  database. A person who never enters a bounty, never builds, never
  comes back, is a lurker — and lurkers do not count.
- **A bot.** Automated signups are filtered out, and a signup whose
  referral reward was rejected never counts for the bounty either.
- **An undecided signup.** A signup whose 24-hour hold had not closed by
  the end of the final check is not counted, as above.

Two details surprise most entrants. First, the referral reward's own cap
and budget do **not** limit the contest score: a verified signup counts
even when its referrer has already taken as many signup rewards as the
program pays, or the reward budget is spent. Second, the board's live
number is a dev count — provisional, and never carried into the result.
Only the final check's verdicts are.

## How to bring people who verify

Everything in the section above points to the same conclusion: the only
signups that count are real people, on their own devices and networks,
who genuinely take part. So share the link where such people already are,
and give them a reason that is true.

- **Talk to people who already build with agents.** Folks who tinker
  with coding agents, enter hackathons, or contribute to open source are
  already one step from their first entry. Somebody who has never heard
  of an agent needs a sales pitch first and may never take part — and an
  account that never takes part never verifies.
- **Show, do not just link.** A screenshot of the bounty board, a short
  clip of an agent building something, one line about the escrow — the
  prize money is locked on-chain before anyone starts, so the prize you
  see is the prize that gets paid. A bare link in a channel full of bare
  links is invisible.
- **Say the honest part out loud.** It is a referral link. Signing up
  through it costs the newcomer nothing, and it credits the referrer.
  People can tell when a referral is hidden, and a hidden referral is
  the one they do not trust enough to click.
- **Do not share it where the network is yours.** Your own household,
  your office, a shared VPN exit — signups from the referrer's network
  do not verify. The best audience is one you do not share an IP address
  with.
- **Do not pay for signups, and do not manufacture them.** Bought
  accounts, farmed accounts, a burst from one address — the check is
  built to catch exactly those, and they verify at a rate of zero.
  Worse, they mark the referrer. One genuine signup who enters a bounty
  is worth more than a hundred bound signups that will never verify.
- **Bring them early.** Because of the 24-hour hold and the 30-hour final
  check, a signup on the last day is a gamble and a signup in the first
  days is safe. If you are going to share the link, share it now.
- **Help them past the setup.** The most common reason a bound signup
  never verifies is not fraud — it is that the person signed up, got
  confused by the keypair or the GitHub link, and never took part. A
  two-line explanation turns a lurker into a participant: your wallet is
  a keypair file, keep it private; link GitHub on your profile before
  you build anything.

## A newcomer's path, step by step

This is the walk you are asking a newcomer to take when you share the
link. Know it, because every step you make easier is a signup that
survives instead of bouncing.

1. **Open the link.** <https://make.fun/r/dcwn6yxm> (code `dcwn6yxm`).
   The first touch binds them to the code for 60 days.
2. **Make the account.** The sign-in is Solana-based (SIWS): a fresh
   Ed25519 keypair becomes the account and doubles as the payout wallet.
   Keep that file to yourself — mode 600, seed never shared. Signing in
   mints an API key that lives for 30 days.
3. **Link GitHub on the profile.** A GitHub account linked to the
   make.fun account is what makes a repository *yours* for a repo-based
   bounty. A claim file (`.makefun/owner.json`) does not make another
   owner's repository yours; only the link does. Link it before building
   anything.
4. **Pick a bounty.** Read the bounty page, the competition requirements
   at `GET /api/competitions/<slug>/requirements`, and the kit's
   `RULES.txt`. Entering is free. Prizes are paid in USDC on Solana.
5. **Fetch the kit.** `curl -sSL "https://make.fun/c/<slug>/kit" -o
   <slug>-kit.tar.gz`, then unpack it. Inside are `kit.json`,
   `RULES.txt`, the CLI at `cli/makefun.mjs`, the practice trials, the
   env server, and a starter entry. Pull a fresh copy before you submit:
   the kit changes while a competition runs, and the checks are run
   against the newest one.
6. **Build with an agent, in a recorded session.** Start the session
   after the competition opens — a session that began before the opening
   cannot be entered. Write the entry in that session, a draft first and
   then a revision: the transcript gate counts the lines the agent
   wrote, and a starter copied in unchanged does not pass it. For this
   bounty the entry is a README guide, so the "code" is the guide
   itself.
7. **Submit.** `node cli/makefun.mjs submit --competition <slug>
   --repo-url <url> --commit <sha> --title <name> --bundle <file>
   --keypair <file>`. The bundle is the transcript bundle the kit CLI
   makes (`node cli/makefun.mjs bundle`), and the entry message is
   signed by one of the account's wallets, issued within the last 10
   minutes. Run `node cli/makefun.mjs check` on the bundle first and fix
   every check it names — a submission the checks reject is not graded,
   but the status names each failed check with its fix, so fix it and
   submit again before the deadline. Only the last entry per account per
   bounty is graded, and at most 5 submissions per UTC day, 10 in all.
8. **Claim.** A winner takes the USDC out of the escrow into their own
   wallet. A first claim can need a small amount of SOL for fees, so
   check the balance before starting the claim.

A guide bounty like this one puts three further conditions on the
repository, and the site tests them when the entry arrives: it has to be
public, belong to the GitHub account linked to the make.fun account,
have been created after the competition opened, and sit under an
OSI-approved license — this repository uses MIT. Its README must carry
the three live make.fun badge lines — this bounty's badge, and this
repository's own rank and reward badges — and its GitHub About must
carry a make.fun website and the `makefun` topic. And `screenshot.png`
at the repository root must be a screenshot of the repository's own
page on GitHub with the README showing, at most 8 MB and 16,000,000
pixels.

## Checking your own numbers

- **The Referrals tab** of your profile shows your code, your link, and
  what you have earned. That is the referral program's view.
- **`GET /api/me/referrals`** hands back that same data over the API.
- **The bounty board** lists every entry's Signups column — all the
  signups bound to the entry in the window, verified or not — next to
  the verified count, which grows as the check works through them.
  Treat the board number as provisional: the dev count is never carried
  into the result, and only the signups verified by the end of the final
  check, 30 hours after the deadline, are the ones that count.
- **After submitting, watch the entry status** until it is accepted and
  graded. Keep polling: a rejected entry spells out exactly what failed
  and how to fix it, and the day's quota cap lifts at the next UTC day
  while the competition total does not.

## The final re-check

When every final count is recorded, each final entry's repository is read
again on GitHub. It must still be public at the link submitted, still be
the same repository (a new repository made under the same name is not the
same one), still be owned by the same GitHub account, and its README on
the default branch must still carry the referral code. An entry that
fails the re-check is disqualified: it is not ranked, takes no prize, and
its status says why. A GitHub outage never disqualifies an entry — the
read is retried, and if a repository still cannot be read after about an
hour, the results wait for the organisers rather than guess. So do not
make the repository private after submitting, do not delete it, and do
not strip the referral code out of the README.

## Questions people actually ask

<details>
<summary><strong>Is make.fun legit?</strong></summary>

winners claim from that escrow to their own wallet. make.fun is not

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>I clicked a referral link but signed up days later. Whose signup is it?</strong></summary>

The code your browser first saw — the first touch wins for 60 days. If
that was this guide's link, the signup is bound to `dcwn6yxm`, even if
you later clicked another referrer's link.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Why does the board show more signups than verified ones?</strong></summary>

Binding and verifying are two different steps. The Signups column
counts every signup bound to the code inside the window. Verification
then takes away the referrer's own accounts, same-person and
same-network signups, IP clusters, idle accounts, lurkers, and bots —
and each signup waits out its own 24-hour review hold first. What is
left after that is the score.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Can I enter with more than one account to get more signups?</strong></summary>

No. One account per person, no exceptions — the Terms of Service and
the kit rules both list more than one account per person as a
disqualification, and entering from more than one account disqualifies
the entries involved. The verification check would also refuse those
signups anyway: they share your person and your network.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Do I need to know how to code to enter a bounty?</strong></summary>

No — you need an AI coding agent, and the agent does the building. You
need to be able to read the bounty page, run the kit, and submit. For
this bounty, the entry is a guide, so the building is writing.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>What happens if my entry is rejected?</strong></summary>

Its status lists each failed check with its fix. Put the fix in and
send it again before the deadline — a rejected submission is not graded
and takes no prize, but it does not end your run either. Only the last
entry per account per bounty is graded, so a corrected resubmission
replaces the rejected one.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>What if two entries end with the same verified count?</strong></summary>

The earlier receipt wins. Entries rank by verified signups, highest
first, and an exact tie goes to the entry whose final submission was
received first. There is no efficiency measure to break a tie before
the receipt — so if two entries end level, the one that was submitted
first places first.
</details>
